The Ship of Theseus

A philosophical puzzle about identity, change, and what makes something the same over time.

📹 CASE TRAILER
🎧 AUDIO NARRATION

The Ship of Theseus is one of the most enduring philosophical paradoxes, dating back to ancient Greece. It asks: if an object has all its parts replaced over time, is it still the same object?

The paradox is named after the mythical Greek hero Theseus, who returned from Crete on a ship that was preserved in the Athenian harbor. As the ship aged, its wooden planks were gradually replaced. Eventually, every part of the original ship had been replaced.

The Paradox:

If all the parts of the ship have been replaced, is it still the Ship of Theseus? Philosophers have proposed various solutions:

1. Material Continuity: The ship is defined by its physical components. Since all parts have been replaced, it is no longer the same ship.

2. Functional Continuity: The ship is defined by its form and function. As long as it maintains the same shape and purpose, it remains the Ship of Theseus.

3. Causal Continuity: The ship is defined by its history and continuity of existence. The replacement parts are causally connected to the original, so it is still the same ship.

The Extended Paradox:

The paradox becomes more complex when we consider what happens to the replaced parts. If someone collects all the original planks and rebuilds the ship, which is the "real" Ship of Theseus—the one in the harbor or the reconstructed one?

Modern Applications:

The Ship of Theseus paradox has implications for:

• Personal Identity: Are we the same person if all our cells are replaced over time?

• Artifact Preservation: When restoring historical objects, how much can be replaced before it ceases to be authentic?

• Software and Digital Objects: Is a program still the same if all its code is rewritten?

• Corporations: If a company replaces all its employees and assets, is it still the same company?

The paradox reveals the complexity of identity and continuity. It challenges our intuitive understanding of what makes something "the same" over time, forcing us to confront the nature of change and persistence.

Your investigation explores the boundaries of identity. What defines an object's essence? When does change become transformation?
